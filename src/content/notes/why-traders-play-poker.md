Tournaments are exciting. Cash games are where the real work is. The depth of decisions in a cash game — pot geometry, range construction, exploitative vs. GTO lines — is a different beast from tournament poker, where stack dynamics and ICM pressure dominate thinking.

**Decisions per hour.** Cash games run deeper and faster in terms of meaningful spots. You're playing more hands, making more close decisions, and getting real-time feedback on whether your reads and lines are working.

**Variance tolerance.** You can play perfectly and lose for weeks. Managing that — staying process-oriented, not results-oriented — is the same muscle you use in trading. The edge is real; the variance around it is brutal.

**Probabilistic thinking.** Every decision is a distribution, not a point. The best play with two aces can still lose to garbage. The habit of thinking in probabilities, not outcomes, is genuinely transferable.

**Exploitative play.** Game theory gives you a baseline. But most players have exploitable leaks — bet-sizing tells, range imbalances, tilt patterns. Finding and adjusting to those is where the money is at recreational stakes.

## The overlap

It's not random that trading desks are full of poker players. The overlap is structural:

- Thinking in EV, not outcomes
- Staying calm in losing runs
- Reading patterns and adjusting
- Bet sizing as information
- Position and timing
- Exploiting systematic leaks
- Bankroll management

## A note on ICM

I made the final table of the IITD college tournament and finished third. Multi-table format — the variance is higher, the ICM math kicks in, and surviving deep runs requires adjusting strategy as stacks shorten. Bubbled a few spots I shouldn't have, and learned the hard way what ICM pressure actually feels like when it's your money on the line.

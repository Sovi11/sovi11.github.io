Gaming has always been less about the game and more about the meta — understanding the system, finding the edge, executing under pressure.

## Clash Royale

Real-time strategy with perfect information asymmetry — you don't know your opponent's deck until they play cards. Deck building, counter-meta reads, and split-second execution all matter. The ladder is a real competitive ecosystem.

12,000+ trophies puts you in the top percentile of global players. The ladder at this level is solved-ish — the variance is low, and consistent wins require genuine understanding of the meta, matchups, and tempo.

Third place in the IITD college tournament. Single-elimination at that stage — variance is high, execution pressure is real. One wrong card placement in a tight match and it's over.

## Chess

Competed at zonal and state level during school. Chess is where the pattern recognition habit started — reading positions, thinking in trees, playing the player as much as the board. It is also why [Hollow Chess](https://hollowchess.com) exists: I wanted a version where the board itself was a piece.

## The thread

Every game has rules, incentives, and emergent dynamics. Finding out how those interact — faster than other players — is the actual game.

And late in a tournament, with something at stake, every decision gets heavier. Getting comfortable in that feeling is something you only learn by being in it.
